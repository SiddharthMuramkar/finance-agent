# How serious programmes order finance and economics

Research notes gathered on 2026-11-01. Prices, limits and terms change, so recheck before relying on them. This is not legal advice.

## Summary

None of the seven programmes is a complete beginner-to-rigorous map on its own. CFA Level I is the broadest but assumes introductory statistics, economics and accounting. MIT 15.401 calls itself rigorous and its current catalog lists no prerequisite, but its outline omits accounting, central banks and currencies. Yale and CORE teach money, banks and crises but little accounting or equity valuation. OpenStax Finance is the only finance sequence with its own accounting, statistics and economics chapters, yet its preface places it below "more rigorous upper-level courses". NISM is a set of role-based exams, not a ladder, and the only programme built around Indian markets. Most programmes agree on four orderings: present value before pricing, derivatives after bonds and equities, monetary policy before exchange rates, and financial statements before equity valuation. They disagree on the starting point, bonds or equities first, when risk theory arrives, and micro or macro first. Venture finance is the thinnest area everywhere. No published programme uses the hybrid in the options, and this research did not cover outcome evidence comparing orders.

## Options

### A. CFA order with its assumed foundations first

- **Description:** Start with introductory statistics, economics and accounting. Then follow CFA: Quant, Economics, Corporate Finance, FSA, Equities, Fixed Income, Derivatives, Alternatives, Portfolio, Ethics. Add extra macro depth and venture finance as separate modules.
- **Evidence:** Broadest published coverage of bonds, FSA, valuation, FX and corporate finance. CFA Institute's material says the programme assumes introductory statistics, economics and accounting, which gives the entry bar. Over 300 hours per level is the only workload figure found. Kaplan Schweser (third-party) recommends this order for candidates with no prior knowledge.
- **Weakness:** Built for exam coverage and investment careers, and Economics is only 6-9% of Level I. Equities come before bonds, against MIT, OpenStax Finance and Yale. WACC comes before the CAPM module. I found no CFA Institute statement that the outline order is a study order. Outlines do not name venture capital. CFA material is marked not for distribution, so it may not suit the private library.

### B. Money and macro first

- **Description:** CORE micro 1-2 and 8-9, then CORE macro 1-5, then the financial sector (macro 6), global economy (macro 7) and crises (macro 8). Then Yale-style institutions: debt, stocks, banks, futures, options. Statements and equity valuation come last.
- **Evidence:** Shiller says ECON 252 presumes only Intro Econ. CORE publishes strict prerequisites for macro 1-5 and 5 before 7, and says macro before micro is possible. Both cover central banks and crises strongly. This route matches the learner's own words about 'basically learning economics'.
- **Weakness:** Late arrival at what Damodaran-style reading needs. CORE has no statements content in its index and treats bond prices in an extension. Yale has 0 'income statement' and 0 'price-earnings' hits, its maths review sessions are not on Open Yale, and its lectures date from 2011.

### C. Accounting, then present value, then instruments (OpenStax Finance)

- **Description:** Governance, money and rates, accounting, statements, ratios, time value, bonds, stocks, market history, statistics, regression, portfolio and CAPM, capital budgeting, capital structure, forecasting, working capital, risk management. Pair with OpenStax Economics or CORE for macro.
- **Evidence:** The only finance sequence with its own accounting, statistics and economics chapters. The preface gives a chapter table and says later chapters build on earlier core chapters. It is free and spreadsheet-based, and it agrees with the present-value and statements-before-valuation orderings.
- **Weakness:** Self-described as aimed at conceptual accessibility and preparing for more rigorous later courses, so it would need a harder layer to meet the learner's stated goal. No derivatives chapter, 0 'venture capital' hits, passing central-bank coverage, and US-focused (chapter 12 covers US markets; 0 RBI hits).

### D. Asset-pricing spine (MIT)

- **Description:** Present value, fixed income, equities, forwards and futures, options, risk and return, portfolio theory, CAPM and APT, capital budgeting, efficient markets. Run accounting alongside (15.501-style), then corporate finance (15.402-style), then venture finance (15.431-style). Add macro separately.
- **Evidence:** Syllabus calls it a rigorous introduction, the catalog lists no prerequisite for 15.401 or 15.501, and MIT publishes a clear chain from 15.401 to 15.402 to 15.431. Exams draw largely from a public problem collection, which could help quiz design.
- **Weakness:** Outline has no statements, central banks or currencies, so most of the economics goal needs other sources. The material is graduate level, from 2008, and built around a commercial textbook. 'No prerequisite' at MIT does not show it suits a complete beginner; a suitable maths on-ramp is unverified.

### E. Hybrid built from the agreed orderings

- **Description:** 1 Algebra, compounding, present value, probability. 2 Intro micro and macro, money, banks, central banks. 3 Rates, government bonds, yields, term structure, duration. 4 FX and global capital flows. 5 Financial statements. 6 Credit analysis and equity valuation. 7 Risk, portfolio theory, CAPM. 8 Capital budgeting, WACC, capital structure, then venture finance. 9 Derivatives. 10 Crises and behaviour, then capstone reading of target writers. Indian market material from NISM (XII, XV, IV, I) at each stage. My inference: this could be stored as a prerequisite graph, as CORE's published prerequisites and OpenStax's modular design suggest, rather than a single line.
- **Evidence:** Each main edge has a cited source. Present value before pricing: MIT, OpenStax Finance, CFA, Yale. Monetary policy before FX: CORE, CFA, OpenStax Economics. Statements before equity valuation: OpenStax Finance, CFA, NISM XV. Bonds before equities: MIT, OpenStax Finance, Yale. CAPM before capital budgeting: MIT, OpenStax Finance. Derivatives after underlying assets: MIT, CFA, Yale, NISM XII. Venture after corporate finance: MIT's 15.431 chain. Economics before accounting: CFA, OpenStax Finance. Credit after statements follows CFA, where credit analysis sits after FSA.
- **Weakness:** No published programme uses this exact order, so it is untested. It overrides CFA on bonds versus equities and on corporate finance timing, and overrides Yale's early risk teaching. Derivatives after corporate finance is my placement; MIT places them before it. Venture and India content must be sourced separately because no programme covers venture finance in depth. Fit to reading Ed Zitron is an inference, not verified.

## Findings

- CFA Level I topic order in the 2027 outline PDF: Quantitative Methods, Economics, Corporate Finance, Financial Statement Analysis, Equities, Fixed Income, Derivatives, Alternative Investments, Portfolio Construction, Ethics. The 2026 outline uses the same order (https://www.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/docs/programs/cfa-program/2026-l1-topics-combined.pdf). The exam page lists a different session order: Ethics, Quant, Economics, FSA, Corporate Finance, then Equities, Fixed Income, Derivatives, Alternatives, Portfolio. 2027 weights: Ethics 10-15%; Quant, FSA, Equities and Fixed Income 11-14% each; Economics, Corporate Finance, Derivatives and Alternatives 6-9% each; Portfolio 8-12% (https://www.cfainstitute.org/programs/cfa-program/candidate-resources/level-i-exam). Source: https://www.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/2027levelitopicoutline_online.pdf
- CFA prerequisites: enrolment needs a bachelor's degree, undergraduate status within the stated window, or 4,000 hours of work and/or higher education, with no subject requirement (https://www.cfainstitute.org/programs/cfa-program/policies). A 2023 CFA Institute slide deck says that from 2024 the programme assumes knowledge from introductory university courses in statistics, economics and accounting, moved into prerequisite readings. For 2027 the Quant prerequisite reading is retired (https://www.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/curriculum-update-2027.pdf), while Economics and FSA prerequisite readings still appear in the 2027 notice (https://www.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/docs/programs/cfa-program/candidate-resources/2027-cfa-program-level-1-notice.pdf). Successful candidates report over 300 study hours per level. Source: https://higherlogicdownload.s3.amazonaws.com/CFAI/99078d55-3462-4c4e-9bcf-21e4f8fea430/UploadedImages/documents/CFA_Stamford_15_June_2023__1__new.pdf
- Third-party source, not CFA Institute: Kaplan Schweser recommends the same order as the CFA outline PDF for anyone without prior knowledge, saying some topics build on others, for example asset valuation on time value of money. It adds that the order may not matter much for those with the knowledge. Source: https://www.schweser.com/cfa/blog/how-to-pass-the-cfa-exam/best-cfa-level-1-study-order (not confirmed in a primary source)
- MIT 15.401 (Fall 2008, Andrew Lo, Graduate level) has four parts. Lecture order: Introduction, Present Value, Fixed-Income, Equities, Forwards and Futures, Options, Risk and Return, Portfolio Theory, CAPM and APT, Capital Budgeting, Efficient Markets (https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-401-finance-theory-i-fall-2008/pages/lecture-notes/). The syllabus calls it a 'rigorous introduction' and states no prerequisites (https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-401-finance-theory-i-fall-2008/pages/syllabus/). The current catalog entry for 15.401 Managerial Finance says 'Prereq: None' (https://catalog.mit.edu/subjects/15/). Source: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-401-finance-theory-i-fall-2008/fc2d55329f1bf7e5af23b4724ee594c2_MIT15_401F08_courseOutline.pdf
- Yale ECON 252 (Shiller, recorded 2011) lecture order: risk and crises, invention in finance, diversification, insurance, Swensen guest, efficient markets, debt, stocks, real estate, behavioural finance, misbehaviour and regulation, banks, Greenberg guest, futures, Cha guest, options, monetary policy, investment banks, money managers, exchanges, public and non-profit finance, purpose. The syllabus requires 'some facility with elementary algebra and calculus' and says exams are roughly 50% math and theory. In Lecture 1 Shiller says it presumes only Intro Econ, keeps maths to a minimum, leaves theory to ECON 251, and that the maths review sessions are not on Open Yale (https://oyc.yale.edu/economics/econ-252-11/lecture-1). Source: https://oyc.yale.edu/economics/econ-252
- CORE The Economy 2.0 is two volumes for 'any first course in economics'. Micro units: prosperity and inequality, technology, scarcity and choice, strategic interaction, institutions, firm and employees, firm and customers, supply and demand, lenders and borrowers, market failure (https://books.core-econ.org/the-economy/microeconomics/0-3-contents.html). Macro units: labour-market supply side, unemployment and institutions, aggregate demand, inflation, macro policy, financial sector, policy in the global economy, financial and environmental crises, development, government (https://books.core-econ.org/the-economy/macroeconomics/0-3-contents.html). Strict prerequisites: micro 1 before 2 and 7 before 8; macro 1 before 2, 1-3 before 4, 4 before 5, 5 before 7. It says macro before micro is 'quite possible'. Source: https://books.core-econ.org/the-economy/macroeconomics/0-5-note-to-instructors.html
- OpenStax Principles of Finance order, with its own core or extension labels: 1 Intro (core), 2 Governance (core), 3 Money and Rates (extension), 4 Accrual Accounting (extension), 5 Statements (core), 6 Financial Health (core), 7-9 Time Value (core), 10 Bonds (core), 11 Stocks (core), 12 US Market History (core), 13-14 Statistics and Regression (extension), 15 Investing and CAPM (core), 16 Capital Budgeting (core), 17 Raising Capital (core), 18 Forecasting (core), 19 Working Capital (extension), 20 Risk Management (extension). The extension chapters supply prerequisite accounting, statistics and economics. Target is second-year business students, and chapters 'generally build on' earlier core chapters. Source: https://openstax.org/books/principles-finance/pages/preface
- OpenStax Principles of Economics 3e uses 'the traditional progression from microeconomics to macroeconomics' in eight parts: What is Economics (1-2), Supply and Demand including labour and financial markets (3-4), Micro Theory (5-10), Micro Policy including financial markets (11-18), Macro Goals including trade and capital flows (19-23), AD/AS (24-26), Monetary and Fiscal Policy (27-31), International (32-34). I found no stated prerequisite. Chapter 1 sends readers to a maths appendix and says 'we will mostly use graphs' (https://assets.openstax.org/oscms-prodcms/media/documents/principles-economics-3e_-_WEB.pdf). Source: https://openstax.org/books/principles-economics-3e/pages/preface
- NISM is not an ordered programme. Its exams are mostly SEBI-mandated benchmarks for specific market roles (https://www.nism.ac.in/certifications), and its FAQ says there is no age or educational eligibility requirement (https://certifications.nism.ac.in/nismaol/FAQ.htm). Series-XII Securities Markets Foundation is the entry exam, in this order: markets, securities and asset allocation, primary markets, secondary markets, mutual funds, derivatives (https://www.nism.ac.in/curriculum-securities-markets-foundation). Series-XV Research Analyst order: profession, markets, terminology, research approaches, economy, industry, company, financial analysis, corporate actions, valuation, commodities, risk and return, research reports, regulation, technical analysis. Source: https://www.nism.ac.in/curriculum-research-analyst-certification
- Bonds and yields, by depth of published outline. CFA has about 20 Fixed Income modules: features, issuance, prices and yields, spreads, spot, par and forward curves, duration, convexity, credit, securitisation, ABS and MBS. MIT covers markets, term structure, conventions, hedging interest-rate risk, inflation and credit risk. OpenStax Finance chapter 10 covers valuation, yield curve and default risk (https://openstax.org/books/principles-finance/pages/10-why-it-matters). Yale Lecture 8 covers bond pricing, forward rates and expectations theory (https://oyc.yale.edu/economics/econ-252-11/lecture-8). OpenStax Economics teaches bond yield in 17.2 with 0 yield-curve hits. CORE defines bonds in 6.3 and 6.9, with bond prices in Extension 6.11 and no 'yield' index entry. NISM's dedicated Fixed Income exam ended 1 October 2024; Series-IV opens with term structure, spot rate, YTM and risk measures (https://www.nism.ac.in/curriculum-interest-rate-derivatives/). Source: https://www.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/2027levelitopicoutline_online.pdf
- Central banks and rates. CFA has a Monetary Policy module on central bank roles, tools, the transmission mechanism and targeting. Yale Lecture 18 covers central banking history, the federal funds rate and reserve and capital requirements (https://oyc.yale.edu/economics/econ-252-11/lecture-18). OpenStax Economics chapters 27-28 cover money, banking, central bank execution and monetary pitfalls. CORE covers inflation targeting (5.9-5.10), transmission (5.13), the central bank and its balance sheet (6.6-6.7) and money creation (6.8). OpenStax Finance mentions central banks only in passing, for example around the yield curve, with 8 hits. MIT's outline has no central bank topic. NISM XV has one objective on monetary policy (5.3.7) and lists RBI under regulators; XII lists RBI only as a regulator (https://www.nism.ac.in/test-objectives-research-analyst-w-e-f-january-20-2026). Source: https://www.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/2027levelitopicoutline_online.pdf
- Currencies and global markets. CFA has modules on Geopolitics, International Trade, Capital Flows and the FX Market, and Exchange Rate Calculations including covered-interest arbitrage. CORE has 5.14 on monetary policy and the exchange rate and all of macro Unit 7, including exchange-rate regimes and global capital mobility. OpenStax Economics has chapter 29 on exchange rates plus chapters 23, 33 and 34 on trade. OpenStax Finance has 3.5 and 20.3 (https://openstax.org/books/principles-finance/pages/3-why-it-matters). NISM Series-I covers FX markets and currency derivatives (https://www.nism.ac.in/curriculum-currency-derivatives/). MIT's outline has no currency topic. Yale has no lecture titled on currencies, and my search of all 23 transcripts found 'exchange rate' only twice, both in Lecture 3. Source: https://www.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/2027levelitopicoutline_online.pdf
- Accounting and financial statements. CFA FSA runs: introduction, income statement, balance sheet, cash flow I and II, inventories, long-term assets, liabilities and equity, taxes, reporting quality, ratios, modelling; questions are IFRS-based unless stated. OpenStax Finance has chapters 4-6. NISM XV chapter VIII covers balance sheet, P&L, cash flows, notes, audit report, ratios and DuPont. MIT's 15.401 outline has none, and accounting is a separate course with no prerequisite (15.501, https://catalog.mit.edu/subjects/15/). Yale discusses balance sheets of Xerox and Microsoft in Lecture 9 (https://oyc.yale.edu/economics/econ-252-11/lecture-9) with 0 'income statement' hits across transcripts. OpenStax Economics has 0 'financial statement' hits. CORE's index has no accounting entry apart from growth accounting. Source: https://www.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/2027levelitopicoutline_online.pdf
- Valuation. CFA 2027 Equities covers dividend discount, FCFE, FCFF, residual income, multiples and statement forecasting, and the 2027 update says valuation is now grounded in forecasting with scenario analysis (https://www.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/curriculum-update-2027.pdf). MIT covers DCF, EPS and P/E, then binomial and Black-Scholes option pricing. OpenStax Finance has chapter 11 on stocks and 0 Black-Scholes hits. NISM XV chapter X covers price versus value, DCF, relative valuation and sum-of-the-parts; XIX-C chapter XIV covers fund portfolio valuation (https://www.nism.ac.in/curriculum-alternative-investment-fund-managers). Yale derives present values for bonds in Lecture 8 and has 0 'price-earnings' hits. CORE treats present value and asset prices in 5.12 and an extension. Source: https://www.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/2027levelitopicoutline_online.pdf
- Corporate finance is well covered and venture finance is not. Corporate: CFA covers governance, working capital, NPV, IRR, ROIC, real options, WACC, Modigliani-Miller and business models. MIT covers capital budgeting and real options. OpenStax Finance covers chapters 2 and 16-19. Venture: MIT puts 15.431 Entrepreneurial Finance and Venture Capital two courses later, after 15.402 or an equivalent. OpenStax Economics has a short passage in 17.1. OpenStax Finance has 0 'venture capital' hits. CFA's 2026 and 2027 outlines have 0 'venture' hits, with only a Private Capital module. In Yale the term appears only in Lectures 6 and 19, which are the Swensen guest lecture and the lecture with a guest speaker. CORE's indexes have no venture entry. NISM XV lists venture funds as market participants, and the XIX-C and XIX-D curricula have 0 'venture' hits. Source: https://catalog.mit.edu/subjects/15/

CFA puts Time Value of Money in Quant, ahead of Equities and Fixed Income. Yale derives present values at the start of its debt lecture, before the stocks lecture. Exception: NISM XII lists DCF and relative valuation objectives (2.12) before time value of money (2.13) (https://www.nism.ac.in/test-objectives-w-e-f-dec-15-2020). Yale has futures at Lecture 15 and options at Lecture 17, after debt and stocks. NISM XII has derivatives as its last unit. NISM XV has chapter VIII before chapter X. Equities first: CFA (Equities before Fixed Income), NISM XII (2.3 before 2.4, https://www.nism.ac.in/test-objectives-w-e-f-dec-15-2020), NISM XV (equity terminology before debt). CORE micro starts with prosperity and inequality, and CORE macro starts with the labour market. OpenStax Finance starts with finance basics and governance. OpenStax Economics starts with scarcity. NISM XII starts with market structure and participants. Source: https://oyc.yale.edu/economics/econ-252
- Disagreement: timing of risk theory. Yale teaches diversification (Lecture 4) and efficient markets (Lecture 7) before debt and stocks. MIT teaches portfolio theory and CAPM after derivatives, then capital budgeting, and efficient markets last. OpenStax Finance has CAPM (15) before capital budgeting (16) and WACC (17). - Disagreement: order within economics. OpenStax Economics goes micro then macro, and its macro has money (27-28) before fiscal policy (30-31). CFA opens with market structures, then business cycles, fiscal policy, monetary policy, geopolitics, trade and FX, so fiscal comes before monetary. CORE says macro can precede micro and builds macro from labour supply, then demand, inflation and policy, with the financial sector only at Unit 6. OpenStax Finance targets 'conceptual accessibility' and prepares students for 'more rigorous upper-level courses' (https://openstax.org/books/principles-finance/pages/preface). ## Still unknown

- No study compared learning outcomes across these orders, because I did not search for one. All evidence here is published structure, not proof that one order teaches better.
- Whether CFA Institute itself recommends a study order. I found only Kaplan Schweser's third-party recommendation; the outline order may be presentation only.
- CFA full curriculum text was not read, only topic outlines and learning outcomes. Venture capital may appear inside the Private Capital module without being named in the outline.
- The statement that CFA assumes introductory statistics, economics and accounting comes from a 2023 slide deck about 2024, hosted on a CFA Institute society file store. I did not find the same wording on cfainstitute.org for 2027, though the 2027 notice still lists Economics and FSA prerequisite readings.
- Whether MIT's Fall 2008 class had prerequisites. The OCW syllabus states none; 'Prereq: None' comes from the current catalog for 15.401 Managerial Finance, which may differ from the 2008 course.
- Yale's stated prerequisites conflict: the syllabus requires algebra and calculus, while Lecture 1 mentions only Intro Econ. I did not review ECON 251, the Fabozzi textbook, or Yale's problem sets.
- All term-hit counts are my text searches of PDFs, transcripts, outlines and indexes. A zero means the term was not found, not that the concept is absent. Yale counts exclude assigned readings.
- NISM coverage is based on curricula for XII, XV, IV, I, XIX-C and XIX-D (a term search only for XIX-D), plus XVI. I did not read X-A, X-B, IX Merchant Banking or XXI-B, or the study workbooks, so workbook depth and rigour are unassessed. The NISM FAQ page carries no date that I saw.
- The OpenStax Principles of Finance details page says a new edition is available, but I found no later edition. This may be page boilerplate.
- Licences for CORE Econ and Open Yale Courses, and terms for NISM workbooks, were not checked.
- Which topics are needed to read Ed Zitron or Aswath Damodaran was not mapped here, so fit to those goals is inference.
- How well any route suits a learner with no maths background was not tested; no programme publishes a maths diagnostic that I reviewed.
