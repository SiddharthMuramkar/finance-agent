# Damodaran and Buffett material: what exists and what the terms say

Research notes gathered on 2026-11-01. Prices, limits and terms change, so recheck before relying on them. This is not legal advice.

## Summary

Damodaran's terms are the loosest. He offers free slides, tests, data, 685 blog posts and an ordered path, under an informal "open site" rule: no resale, please acknowledge. He signed off on two AI bots but gives no formal licence. Buffett's material is tighter. Berkshire's site bars reproduction, and even linking, without written permission. CNBC's owner Versant bars copying and automated extraction, even non-commercially. YouTube allows embedding, not transcript scraping. Buffett publishes no syllabus; The Essays' themed arrangement is the nearest thing. India's Copyright Act allows fair dealing for private use, including electronic storage. That may help a one-learner app, but cloud hosting, contract terms and showing material to others need a lawyer. India material is strong from Damodaran and thin from Buffett.

## Options

### Damodaran-first stored library (slides, tests, blog, data, papers)

- **Fit:** Covers a full ordered course plus India data, under the loosest stated terms of any source here. His stated tolerance includes others using his material to help in teaching, with acknowledgement. Suits one learner and could plausibly extend to a few free users.
- **What would break it:** His rule is informal and could change, and its heading mentions only data and spreadsheets. Charging users, unattributed long quotes, or a name implying approval hits things he objects to. Data vendors may object to redistributed data. In the one packet sampled, about a third of pages lose their content without vision processing. Video needs self-transcription because YouTube bars automated access.
- **Cost:** $0 to download. Valuation packets are about 48 MB. Claude API cost for processing and retrieval not estimated here.

### Berkshire letters stored privately for one learner, short quotes with citations

- **Fit:** About 611,000 words of clean text, which is the core of what Buffett wrote. Fits the existing plan of pulling in a quote or context through RAG when needed. Rests on India's fair dealing exception for private use, which expressly covers electronic storage.
- **What would break it:** Berkshire's terms bar reproduction without written permission. Whether copies on Vercel and SQL, plus passages sent to the Claude API, still count as private use is untested here. Adding any second user removes the private-use argument. Linking to Berkshire's site is also barred on the face of its terms.
- **Cost:** $0 to download. Legal review cost unknown. Embedding and retrieval cost not estimated here.

### Link-out or embed only for CNBC archive and YouTube lectures, no ingestion

- **Fit:** Keeps meeting Q&A and lectures available as sources beside the chat without copying them. YouTube's terms expressly allow the embeddable player. CNBC has time-coded URLs that can open a specific moment.
- **What would break it:** The tutor cannot search or quote what it has not ingested, so it would need hand-built topic-to-timestamp maps, which is itself manual work from the source. URLs may change. I found no linking clause in the Versant text I searched, but I did not read it in full.
- **Cost:** $0 in fees. Curation time is unquantified.

### Ask for written permission (Damodaran, Berkshire, Versant, Cunningham or Harriman House)

- **Fit:** The only route that clearly clears showing material to other users. Damodaran publishes an email address and invites questions, and he has signed off on AI variants before. The Essays front matter offers contact with the editor about use in teaching and training.
- **What would break it:** Berkshire says it cannot provide direct responses to comments, so silence is likely. Versant's terms suggest no easy path. A refusal would leave a written record of the request. Permission from Damodaran may not cover third-party data inside his slides.
- **Cost:** Time and possibly legal fees. Not quantified.

### Paid books outside the library, with the tutor pointing to chapters

- **Fit:** The learner buys The Essays or The Little Book and reads alongside the tutor. Damodaran publishes a chapter-to-session map (https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/valchaptersession.html), and The Essays' themed contents give a reading order.
- **What would break it:** No retrieval over the book text, so no quoting from it through RAG. Chapter pointers must be built and checked by hand against one specific edition. Damodaran says many of his books are overpriced, and availability and price in India were not checked.
- **Cost:** Book prices not checked.

## Findings

- Damodaran site-wide rule. Quote: "This is an open site with very few rules. I have no problem with your use of any of the informatiion on this site but I will only ask two things of you. The first is that you do not try to commercially exploit the data on this site by turning around and selling it... The second is that you acknowledge the parentage when you used the data from this site." The section heading is "Rules on using data and spreadsheets", so its reach over slides and text is ambiguous. I found no copyright or licence notice on 21 of his pages or in the 3 slide PDFs I searched. Private study: appears fine. Showing others: appears tolerated if not sold and if acknowledged, but this is not a formal licence. Source: https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/guide.html
- Damodaran on reuse and AI (Aug 2025). Quote: "I have generally taken a lax view of others borrowing my slides, data, spreadsheets or even webcasts", which he calls "good neighbor sharing" and includes "using my material to help in their teaching". He objects to "reproducing large portions of my writing without acknowledgement" and calls "education companies that used my YouTube videos and charge for classes" scams. On AI: "The Damodaran bot... has access to all of this material, and while I signed off on that and one other variant, there are multiple unauthorized versions". His tools page lists two bots and says "I have no formal relationship" with them (https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/tools.html). Failure point: selling access, large unattributed quotes, or a name implying his approval. Source: https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/08/the-imitation-game-defending-against.html
- NYU MBA and undergraduate classes. 26 to 28 sessions of 80 minutes each. Spring 2026 valuation lecture packets are text-layer PDFs made from PowerPoint: Part 1 is 30.5 MB and 351 pages; Part 2 is 11 MB; Part 3 is 6.1 MB; PPTX versions are linked. Part 1 extracts to only 28,281 words, and 110 of 351 pages have under 15 words, so charts and images are lost without vision processing. The syllabus is an 18-page PDF (https://www.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/eqnotes/eqsyllspr25.pdf). Instructor PowerPoints on book sites need a password from him. Terms: as in the site rule; no notice in the packet text. Source: https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/eqlect.htm
- Free online courses, each with YouTube webcasts and slide PDFs, most with post-class tests. Their own pages state: Foundations of Finance 12 webcasts of 12-20 minutes (the class list says nine sessions, a conflict), Valuation 25, Corporate Finance 36, Investment Philosophies 42, Corporate Life Cycle 20 of 25-35 minutes. Accounting and Statistics are also listed. Sample: Foundations session 6 slides are 15 pages, 4.5 MB, 1,210 words; its test is 2 pages, 398 words, multiple choice. The tests could seed quizzes, but reusing them for others rests on the informal rule only. Source: https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/onlineclass.htm
- Damodaran publishes a teaching sequence. For someone who is "an individual investor trying to find your way in investing, I think that starting with a valuation class, and following up with investment philosophy will yield the biggest payoff". He also says "to value businesses, you need to understand how to run them, making corporate finance a good lead in", and the life cycle class is a "good add on". Accounting, statistics and foundations are an optional "lead-in". A video guide to sequencing is linked from https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/regularclass.htm. Note: built for adults on their own and asynchronous, not for a tutor-led beginner. Source: https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2024/12/for-fun-of-it-open-house-for-my-spring.html
- Beginner prerequisites, in his words. Self-study plan: "Step 1: Accounting statements represent the raw material"; "Step 2: Discounted cash flow valuation is just glorified present value"; then pick a time-based plan. Novices should "strart with the background page" of primers (https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/guide.html). Foundations is "a good first course to take if you have no finance background". The valuation syllabus lists accounting, statistics and finance as preparation. A candidate order is Foundations, Accounting, Statistics, then the Valuation class. My inference, not his words: a beginner also needs percentages, exponents, spreadsheet basics and comfort with US-dollar examples (the one test I sampled is in dollars). Source: https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/background/startval.htm
- Datasets and spreadsheets. 246 Excel links on the current data page, updated yearly (last January 9, 2026; next early January 2027); 73 on the spreadsheets page, which he says to "feel free to modify or adapt". Quote: "I hope you find this data useful and there are no strings attached." Risk: the data derives from Bloomberg, Morningstar, Capital IQ and Compustat. He stopped company-level files after 2018 because "the data providers believe that I am improperly sharing proprietary data" (https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/databreakdown.html). His guide page still mentions company downloads, which is stale. He asks to be left out of court use. Private: fine. Others: appears fine with acknowledgement, not for sale. Source: https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datahistory.html
- Musings on Markets blog. HTML on Blogger, 685 posts per its feed on 2026-11-01, first post September 17, 2008, roughly 4,000 to 5,000 words in the two posts I measured. I found no copyright or licence statement on the home page; Blogger's own terms were not read. He also republishes on Substack and LinkedIn. Private: appears fine. Showing others: short attributed quotes fit his stated views; large unattributed reproduction is what he objects to. Source: https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/
- YouTube lectures. Channel shows 1,416 videos, grouped into class playlists (https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCLvnJL8htRR1T9cbSccaoVw). The one video sampled had only auto-generated captions. Terms: "You may view or listen to Content for your personal, non-commercial use. You may also show YouTube videos through the embeddable YouTube player." Barred: to "reproduce, download... except... with prior written permission" and to "access the Service using any automated means". So embedding works; pulling transcripts into RAG does not on the face of the terms. Alternative: his site links "Video podcast (downloadable)" and audio on NYU Box for Spring 2025 valuation (https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/webcasteqUGspr25.htm), which would need self-transcribing. Source: https://www.youtube.com/t/terms
- Damodaran's books. 12 titles are listed, sold commercially; the site hosts prefaces and support pages (spreadsheets, overheads), not the full text. For investors he suggests The Little Book of Valuation or Narrative and Numbers (https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/valbookcomp.html). He says "you do not need any of my books to take my classes", and of his free papers (46 PDFs linked at https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/papers.html) "if you find the books unaffordable, you can get that material here for free". Wiley publishes at least two of them. Their rights pages were not read, so assume ordinary publisher restrictions until checked. Source: https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/public.htm
- NYU policy on who owns teaching material. Quote: "NYU will not assert ownership of Traditional Works of Scholarship, Lectures and Instructional Media authored, created or given by faculty", except under Section XI.C, and "Instructional Media" includes lecture notes, slides and tests. Damodaran also writes "I am grateful that NYU has allowed me to share my class recordings". This suggests he is the right person to ask, but whether an exception applies to him is unknown. A lawyer question. Source: https://www.nyu.edu/content/dam/nyu/compliance/documents/IPPolicy.pdf
- India-relevant Damodaran material (strong). The current data page has an "Only India" column, with 29 India-specific Excel files covering betas, cost of capital, PE, EV/EBITDA, margins, tax rates and more, though absolute values are "in millions of US dollars". The July 2026 country risk workbook includes India and a Country Lookup sheet. Blog case studies: Zomato (2021 twice, 2022), Paytm (2021), Adani (2023), Walmart and Flipkart (2018). Mumbai seminars with slides and company valuations: Infosys (2018), ITC (2019), "Vedant" (2025), per https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/seminars.htm. The tools page has a Jet India example. Source: https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datacurrent.html
- Berkshire website terms. Quote: "Reproduction or distribution of any materials obtained on this website or linking to this website without written permission is prohibited. All rights reserved." The home page warns "we are unable to provide a direct response" to comments, so getting permission may be hard. A fraud notice says Mr. Buffett "does not currently and will not prospectively endorse investment products" (https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/webfraud.pdf). Private study: reading is the site's purpose; storing a copy relies on a legal exception, not these terms. Showing others: needs written permission on the face of the terms. Source: https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/disclaimer.html
- Berkshire shareholder letters. 48 Buffett letters, 1977-2024: HTML 1977-1997, HTML or PDF 1998-2001, PDF only 2002-2024. All extract cleanly as text, about 611,000 words in total. Stated: "ALL SHAREHOLDER LETTERS INCLUDE COPYRIGHTED MATERIAL REPRODUCED WITH PERMISSION". No copyright line inside the letter text (47 of 48 checked). 1965-1976 letters are not listed; a paid compilation covering 1965-2024 is linked. Greg Abel's 2025 letter (about 10,000 words) sits on a separate page. Terms as in the Berkshire website finding. Source: https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/letters.html
- No Buffett syllabus found. I found none on Berkshire's home page, its letters index, or the CNBC archive menu, which is organised by year, topic, interview and timeline. This is a negative result from those pages only. Nearest Buffett-written primer: An Owner's Manual, HTML, about 5,800 words, stated "Copyright © 1996 By Warren E. Buffett All Rights Reserved". Also on the site: the "Past, Present and Future" special letters and Munger's Wesco letters. A beginner's Buffett track would have to be sequenced by the project itself, likely after accounting basics. My inference: the letters assume terms such as float, book value and GAAP earnings. Source: https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/owners.html
- Buffett partnership letters. Not on Berkshire's site. Third-party copies only: an Ivey Business School PDF, 152 pages, about 75,800 words, text layer, covering letters from 1957, with no rights statement in the text; and rbcpa.com, whose listing says 1959-1975, with the one letter sampled being a scan with no text layer, so it needs OCR (https://www.rbcpa.com/warren-e-buffett/buffett-letters-1959-present). Rights holder not stated. Accuracy against originals is unverified. Private: no stated permission either way. Others: unclear, needs a lawyer or permission. Source: https://www.ivey.uwo.ca/media/2975913/buffett-partnership-letters.pdf (not confirmed in a primary source)
- CNBC Warren Buffett Archive. Stated: "33 full Berkshire Hathaway annual meetings, going back to 1994", "145 hours of searchable video, synchronized to 3000 pages of transcripts", "575 video clips". Format: HTML transcripts beside video; one 1994 session is about 23,600 words; time-coded URLs exist. The terms page defers to the Versant terms (https://versantmedia.com/terms/): "personal and non-commercial use only". Prohibited actions: "No Data Extraction. You may not use any software robots, spider, crawlers..."; no "copy, download... archive... unless expressly permitted by us in writing"; no derivative works "whether for profit or for no profit" (https://www.versantmedia.com/terms/prohibited-actions). Private: watching and reading only. RAG ingestion is barred on its face, even for one person. Source: https://buffett.cnbc.com/
- The Essays of Warren Buffett, selected and arranged by Lawrence Cunningham. Paid book. An 8th edition is listed by Harriman House (06 April 2023), with audiobook and ebook (https://www.harriman-house.com/authors/lawrence-a-cunningham/the-essays-of-warren-buffett-8th-ed/9781804092774). 5th edition front matter: "Copyright © Lawrence A. Cunningham, 1995, 1997, 2001, 2008, 2013, 2015, and 2019" and "Includes previously copyrighted materials, © Warren E. Buffett, reprinted with permission. All rights reserved." Themed order: Prologue on owner principles, then Governance, Investing, Alternatives, Common Stock, Acquisitions, Valuation, Accounting, Taxation, History, Coda, plus a concept glossary. Private: reading a bought copy. Ingesting or showing others: no permission stated. Source: https://cap-press.com/pdf/9781531017507.pdf
- India-relevant Buffett material (thin). The CNBC archive search for India surfaces a 2024 chapter titled "'Loads of opportunities' in India that 'more energetic' future Berkshire management could explore", 2010 chapters "Opportunities in India, but government paralysis is a deterrent" and "Why GEICO isn't looking at China or India", and audience questions from India in 1996 and 2024. In the letters I found only two relevant mentions: 2011 (Marmon's "Indian crane operation") and 2023 (Ajit Jain "born, raised and educated in India"). This came from one search, so it may be incomplete. Source: https://buffett.cnbc.com/warren-buffett-search-results/?query=India
- Law, for flagging only. India's Copyright Act s.52(1)(a): no infringement for "a fair dealing with any work... for the purposes of (i) private or personal use, including research; (ii) criticism or review". Explanation: "The storing of any work in any electronic medium for the purposes mentioned in this clause... shall not constitute infringement". US 17 USC 107 lists "teaching... scholarship, or research" as fair-use purposes, weighed by factors (https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html). Both are copyright exceptions and I found nothing saying they cancel website contract terms. Not legal advice. Lawyer needed before opening to other users, and on the questions listed under unknowns. Source: https://copyright.gov.in/Copyright_Act_1957/chapter_xi.html

## Still unknown

- Wiley's and Harriman House's stated terms, and Damodaran's book copyright pages, were not read. No book front matter was checked except The Essays 5th edition.
- Whether Damodaran's "open site" rule covers slides and blog text, or only data and spreadsheets as its heading says. Only he can settle this.
- Whether NYU's Section XI.C exceptions apply to his recorded classes, given he says NYU "has allowed" sharing.
- Who holds copyright in each Berkshire letter (Buffett or Berkshire), and in CNBC's transcripts as distinct from what was said at the meetings.
- Rights holder of the partnership letters, and whether third-party copies match the originals. I found no official host.
- Lawyer questions: which country's law governs a learner in India using an app hosted abroad; whether website terms override fair dealing or fair use; whether storing on Vercel or SQL and sending passages to the Claude API counts as private use; whether a tutor's own-words summaries are derivative works; what changes when a second user is added.
- The Vercel and database hosting region, which may bear on the governing-law question above. Not checked.
- Licence setting across Damodaran's 1,416 YouTube videos, and caption quality. Only one video was sampled, and no Creative Commons marker was seen on it.
- Whether all CNBC transcripts match the one 1994 session sampled, and the exact basis on which Berkshire released the footage to CNBC.
- The Essays 8th edition's contents and print length. The Harriman House page shows both "873minutes" and "873pages", which looks like a listing glitch, and the contents reported are from the 5th edition.
- Publisher and price of the paid 1965-2024 letters compilation: the link returned no details. Also whether Berkshire ever published the 1965-1976 letters online.
- The full Versant terms and Blogger terms were searched or skipped, not read end to end. Substack and LinkedIn terms for Damodaran's republished posts were not read.
- The 1999 Berkshire letter was word-counted only, not searched for copyright lines or India mentions.
