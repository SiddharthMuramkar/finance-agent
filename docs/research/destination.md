# What a reader needs to understand Zitron and Damodaran

Research notes gathered on 2026-11-01. Prices, limits and terms change, so recheck before relying on them. This is not legal advice.

## Summary

I read 14 pieces, seven per writer, dated February 2025 to October 2026, and checked numbers against Anthropic's S-1, Treasury yields and the New York Fed. The concepts fall into six groups with a clear dependency order: numeracy, the three statements, present value, bonds and spreads, macro rates, cost of capital, then valuation. The writers overlap on cash flow, capex and run rates but otherwise diverge. Zitron's sample has no present-value or cost-of-capital language and relies on credit, contracts and leaks. Damodaran's has no private-credit terms and relies on discounting, risk premiums and published models. Many numbers from both tied to sources, and both have checkable slips, so understanding should include recomputing. Neither sample covers duration, quantitative easing, balance of payments or Indian markets, so these writers cannot define the whole course. Nine of the 14 pieces date from September 2026 onward and most concern AI, which skews the counts. The options give six definitions of the skill.

## Options

### Follow the argument

- **Description:** The learner can read a new piece by either writer without stopping, define each term, restate the chain of reasoning, and say which concept each step depends on.
- **Evidence:** Each of the 14 pieces maps onto the six concept groups. Zitron teaches bonds mid-piece in Z2, while Damodaran assumes the equity risk premium in D2, so the amount of assumed knowledge varies.
- **Weakness:** It does not catch errors. A reader at this level would accept the SOFR gloss in Z2 and the $8bn operating loss in D4.

### Recompute and check against source

- **Description:** The learner takes a claim back to the filing or data series, recomputes it, and identifies mislabels, period mismatches and places where inference goes beyond the text.
- **Evidence:** Many figures tie out, including $974m, $4,585m and 5.29%. Checkable slips also exist in both, including 430% vs 340% in Z1, 0.001 for a basis point in Z3, and $693m vs over $8bn in D4.
- **Weakness:** Arithmetic slips do not settle whether a thesis is right, and the Z1 critique of undefined run rates survives its slip. It depends on filings being available and can turn into pedantry without a forward view.

### Build the valuation yourself

- **Description:** The learner turns a business story into inputs, builds a DCF, varies the assumptions, and defends which outcomes are possible, plausible and probable. This is Damodaran's skill.
- **Evidence:** D4 sets out revenue, margin, reinvestment, cost of capital and failure probability with a spreadsheet and a range of -$373bn to $4.04tn. D2 runs the same logic backwards to get an implied premium, and D5 supplies country inputs.
- **Weakness:** It teaches little credit structure or counterparty tracing. Results depend on asserted inputs and can suggest false precision. It also does not directly answer Zitron-style claims about who pays whom.

### Trace cash and credit

- **Description:** The learner maps who pays whom, derives unit economics from contracts, reprices debt at current yields, and identifies what breaks first. This is Zitron's skill.
- **Evidence:** Z1 derives per-GPU-hour prices from contract totals. Z3 reprices Oracle and CoreWeave bonds. Z5 sums commitments from the S-1. Z4 chains contingencies from token spend to venture funding.
- **Weakness:** The sample has no discount rate, cost-of-capital framework or modelled upside. It relies on leaks and on paywalled material. It sometimes treats missing disclosure as proof. At least one rating forecast is in apparent tension with a reported BB+.

### Adjudicate between them

- **Description:** Given the same facts, the learner can state exactly where the two writers differ, separate factual from framing differences, and name the evidence that would settle each one.
- **Evidence:** Both read the same S-1 on the same day. Both doubt run rates in Z1 and D3 yet reach different conclusions. D3 says a bear thesis built only on losses misses the story, while Z5 says all that matters is what Anthropic is actually doing. The blank payment schedule is one concrete deciding item.
- **Weakness:** It is the hardest to grade, and real outcomes take years. It presupposes the other skills. It can also produce false balance where one side is simply wrong.

### Transfer to any market

- **Description:** The learner applies the same tools without either writer's help to markets they do not cover, such as an Indian government bond, an Indian bank or a currency move.
- **Evidence:** D1, D5 and D7 already work across currencies and countries, and D6 started from an argument about the Indian market. However, 9 of the 14 pieces date from September 2026 onward and most concern AI, and duration, quantitative easing, balance of payments and RBI have no hits. This is the option closest to the learner's stated scope of all finance.
- **Weakness:** Neither writer can define or validate this skill alone, so it needs other source material and a separate standard of correctness. It has the broadest scope and the slowest payoff, and the result may no longer resemble either writer.

## Findings

- Z1, Zitron, 'Annualized Enron Rate' (13 Oct 2026). Argument: AI run-rate figures are marketing, startups pass venture money to the labs, and long-term GPU contracts sit far below spot indices. To follow it: recognised revenue vs run rate, trailing 28 days x 13, gross vs net revenue, consumption vs recurring revenue, gross margin, spot vs contract price, depreciation per GPU-hour, venture rounds. To judge it: matching time periods, useful-life assumptions, whether six contracts represent a market, whether missing disclosure is evidence. Test case: he calls a $48bn run rate 'roughly 430% more' than Q2 revenue. That compares a yearly figure with a quarterly one, and 48/10.9 is 340% more. The S-1 shows unaudited Q2 revenue of $11.555bn, or $46.2bn annualised (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm). From his own figures, his $19.1bn gap computes to $20.1bn and his $1.66bn Harvey total to $1.51bn. Source: https://www.wheresyoured.at/annualized-enron-rate/
- Z2, Zitron, 'Credit Crunch' (6 Oct 2026). Argument: rising Treasury yields plus AI-specific spreads make the debt these companies need unaffordable, and the labs would rate as junk. To follow it: Treasury auctions, price-yield inverse, benchmark rate, spread over same-maturity Treasuries, basis points, investment grade vs junk, EBITDA, SOFR, SPVs, collateral and seniority, convertibles, take-or-pay as debt-equivalent, CLOs, float and at-the-market sales. To judge it: how agencies actually score issuers, whether Uber, Amazon and WeWork are fair comparisons. Test cases: he glosses SOFR as overnight borrowing from the Fed, but the New York Fed defines it as the cost of borrowing cash overnight collateralised by Treasuries (https://www.newyorkfed.org/markets/reference-rates/sofr). He labels $13.07bn as 'losses' and $20.92bn as EBITDA, while his own Z6 lists those as revenue and loss from operations. His yield level fits Treasury's 5.31% on 5 Oct, which fell to 4.94% by 30 Oct (https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?type=daily_treasury_yield_curve&field_tdr_date_value_month=202610). Source: https://www.wheresyoured.at/credit-crunch/
- Z3, Zitron, 'Dead Money' (29 Sep 2026). Argument: hyperscalers' AI revenue is far below capex break-even, most of it comes from two labs, and debt costs create a self-reinforcing 'doom loop'. To follow it: capex break-even, ROIC, building a revenue total from analyst estimates, repricing bonds at current yields, fallen angels, SPV structure, spot vs contract GPU rates, down rounds, opex per dollar of revenue. To judge it: the assumptions inside Goldman's break-even, stacking several firms' estimates, that a repriced bond is a counterfactual because existing coupons do not reset, and that comparing Cognition's valuation with Ford's ignores growth stage. Test cases: he defines a basis point as 0.001, while Z2 correctly says 0.01%. His Reuters-sourced 2025 Anthropic numbers tie to the later S-1: revenue $4,585m and operating expenses $12,645m, or about $2.76 per dollar (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm). He publishes corrections as inline editor's notes. Source: https://www.wheresyoured.at/dead-money/
- Z4, Zitron, 'AI Had Better Work Out' (20 Oct 2026). Argument: AI stocks are a concentrated global risk held by retail investors, and the trade needs a chain of contingencies that must all hold. To follow it: index weights, household equity share, retail ownership, drawdown arithmetic, dot-com history, secured vs unsecured bonds, operating cash flow forecasts, receivables concentration, channel stuffing, TVPI and venture exits. To judge it: contribution arithmetic, adding-up constraints across sectors, and inference from single quotes. Test cases: 28.9% S&P weight x a 20% fall equals the 5.78% index drop of 14 Apr 2000 that he cites, so the headline is weight-times-shock arithmetic rather than a forecast. 'It appears that NVIDIA is channel stuffing' is his inference from a Business Insider passage about Amazon taking racks early. A sidenote says he had tempered his attacks so they would take a back seat to financial analysis and will no longer do so, so a reader must separate analysis from moral argument. Source: https://www.wheresyoured.at/ai-had-better-work-out/
- Z5, Zitron, 'Exit Liquidity' (28 Oct 2026). Argument: the S-1 shows a flattering quarter of a company about to change, so public investors are the exit. To follow it: S-1 structure, risk factors, purchase commitments, finance leases, trailing twelve months, GAAP vs adjusted profit, stock comp, liquidity, customer concentration. To judge it: legal boilerplate vs forecast, what a draft filing normally omits, inference about intent. Verified in the S-1 (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm): Q2 operating income $974m; $112.4bn cash; blank obligations table and run-rate line; commitments of $114.5bn, $110bn, $44.9bn and $161.2bn sum to his $430bn. Where he goes beyond the text: the S-1 passage on 5-7% of revenue says customers violating usage policy or commercial terms, not competitors distilling models. He writes 'guaranteed' worse margins where the S-1 says margins 'may compress'. Source: https://www.wheresyoured.at/exit-liquidity/
- Z6, Zitron, 'Exclusive: OpenAI Losses Increased Nearly 8X in 2025' (15 Jun 2026). A short report on audited statements that he says the FT independently verified. To follow it: income statement lines (revenue $13.07bn, cost of revenue, R&D, S&M, G&A), loss from operations ($20.92bn) vs net loss ($60.35bn before attribution, $38.53bn attributable), fair value changes on convertibles and warrants, related-party payments. To judge it: non-controlling interests and consolidation. He writes of the 2024 attribution 'It's unclear what this means' and 'I will not speculate further', which marks the edge of his accounting analysis in this piece. Source: https://www.wheresyoured.at/exclusive-openai-financials/
- Z7, Zitron, 'AI Is A Money Trap' (6 Aug 2025). Argument: Cursor and similar startups cannot escape through acquisition or IPO, so their failure would hit the labs and data centres. To follow it: annualised revenue (here month x 12, vs 28 days x 13 in Z1), unit economics, capex as share of GDP, free cash flow vs net income, acquisition and IPO exits. To judge it: whether exit options are really closed. He wrote that Cursor must 'die, or get acquired', and Z4 later reports SpaceX buying it in a $60bn all-stock deal. Vocabulary shift: keyword hits for ratings and junk, spreads, bond yields and maturity are 0, 0, 0, 0 here against 31, 8, 17, 9 in Z2. Credit became central to his sample only recently. This piece was read in part; the counts cover the full text. Source: https://www.wheresyoured.at/ai-is-a-money-trap/
- D1, Damodaran, 'Interest Rates and Stock Prices' (10 Sep 2026). Argument: fundamentals rather than the Fed set long rates, and higher rates do not automatically mean lower stock prices because cash flows change too. To follow it: yield curve, inflation + real growth as an 'intrinsic riskfree rate', TIPS breakeven, spreads by rating, bond price sensitivity, discount rate vs cash flow effects, pricing power, carry trade, currency-translated returns. To judge it: whether the proxy is good, whether one year of daily data (169 trading days, split at 3 basis points) supports the claim, and whether Fed debate is really 'pointless'. He also compares aggregate with median returns: tech 25.22% vs median 7.75%. His 4.75% for 31 Aug matches Treasury data (https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?type=daily_treasury_yield_curve&field_tdr_date_value_month=202608). Source: https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/09/interest-rates-and-stock-prices-old.html
- D2, Damodaran, 'Stock Prices, Earnings and Cashflows' (8 Oct 2026). Argument: AI capex at some firms becomes revenue and earnings at others, which lifts aggregate earnings while cash returned to shareholders lags. To follow it: implied equity risk premium as an internal rate of return, capex to assets to book value, amortisation schedules, free cash flow vs earnings, buybacks and net buybacks, joint ventures and off-balance-sheet entities, write-offs. To judge it: assumptions inside the implied premium, public-company-only data, and survivorship (he excludes new listings). Numbers: 8.99% expected return less 5.29% = 3.70%, or 3.92% over a 5.07% dollar riskfree rate. His 5.29% matches Treasury's 30 Sep figure (https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?type=daily_treasury_yield_curve&field_tdr_date_value_month=202609). He discloses his own holdings and rising cash. Source: https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/10/stock-prices-earnings-and-cashflows-ai.html
- D3, Damodaran, 'From Eyeballs to Annual Run Rates' (22 Oct 2026). Argument: young companies are priced rather than valued, on messy metrics, and every such metric still hides assumptions about growth, margins and risk. To follow it: corporate life cycle, value vs price, post-money and forward pricing, multiples and consistency between enterprise value and equity, peer groups, controlling for differences, unit economics, economies of scale. To judge it: whether forward multiples can be compared with current ones, and whether a plausible story is a probable one. Line to test a learner on: 'desperation drives you up the income statement until you find a positive number'. He also says ARR is not standardised and can be gamed, the same doubt as Z1 with a different conclusion. Source: https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/10/from-eyeballs-to-annual-run-rates-arr.html
- D4, Damodaran, 'The Anthropic Prospectus Arrives' (28 Oct 2026). Method: turns a story into inputs: revenue about $1tn by 2036, 27.5% margin, $3 revenue per $1 invested, 9.74% cost of capital, 10% failure chance with half the value lost. Result $1.475tn, range -$373bn to $4.04tn. To follow it: total addressable market, sales-to-capital, cost of capital, operating leverage, present value of commitments as debt, share classes, public benefit corporations, lock-ups. To judge it: which inputs drive value, and how inputs are asserted. Test cases: he gives an H1 operating loss 'over $8 billion', but the S-1 shows $693m; his figure appears to be the $80.7bn net loss less the $72.4bn fair-value change, and most of the remainder is $8.76bn of non-operating other expense (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm). He also says debt including commitments is 'close to half a billion' against commitments of $600bn or more, which looks like a slip for trillion. Source: https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/10/the-anthropic-prospectus-arrives-ipo.html
- D5, Damodaran, 'Country Risk: The 2026 Edition' (15 Jul 2026). Argument: country risk cannot be diversified away and can be converted into a number. To follow it: sovereign ratings, sovereign CDS, mature-market premium, default spread scaled by relative equity volatility (1.55), beta, riskfree rate by currency, inflation differentials, exposure by revenue vs production. To judge it: he says the method has 'very little intellectual firepower' and uses a makeshift approach for unrated countries. Numbers tie: 4.42% - 0.22% = 4.20%, and his Brazil example computes to 12.1% against his 'roughly 12%'. He reports correcting an earlier table. One internal slip: the text says lower Transparency International scores mean less corruption, while his chart legend says low score means most corruption. It is the most directly useful of these pieces for an Indian reader and also covers the Sensex. Source: https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/07/country-risk-drivers-measures-and.html
- D6, Damodaran, 'Return on Equity, Earnings Yield and Market Efficiency' (18 Feb 2025). Written after a LinkedIn thread on the Indian market confused return on equity with earnings yield. Uses two toy businesses of $60m each. To follow it: net income, book equity, return on equity, cost of equity, excess returns, perpetuity value, price to book, earnings yield, market efficiency, good company vs good investment. To judge it: when the no-growth assumption breaks, R&D expensing, ageing assets, fair value accounting. It shows his teaching method of stripping a problem to round numbers, and it makes a ready model for tutor exercises. Source: https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/02/return-on-equity-earnings-yield-and.html
- D7, Damodaran, 'Sovereign Ratings, Default Risk and Markets' (2 Jun 2025). Argument: the Moody's downgrade changes little in markets but changes how analysts must compute the dollar riskfree rate. To follow it: sovereign default, local vs foreign currency debt, rating scales and notches, CDS, debt to GDP, riskfree rate = government bond rate less default spread (4.41% - 0.40% = 4.01%), split of a fixed 8.64% expected return between riskfree rate and premium. To judge it: rating bias, lag and herding, and whether a market non-reaction shows no information. Test case: he calls Baa2 and below speculative grade, while the usual boundary is Baa3/BBB- as investment grade, per Fidelity's table (https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/investment-products/fixed-income-bonds/bond-ratings). The Fidelity page is secondary and I did not check his underlying Moody's table. Source: https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/06/sovereign-ratings-default-risk-and.html

- What Zitron does with evidence. Sources: leaked audited statements, SEC filings and contract disclosures, The Information, FT, WSJ, Reuters, sell-side notes, Ramp data, and pseudonymous analysts, sometimes hedged ('If Irrational Analysis is right'). Methods: converts run rates into implied period revenue; derives price per GPU-hour from contract totals, for example $165.2m / 1,392 GPUs / 36 months = $4.52, which recomputes correctly; adds up analyst estimates and compares them with break-even; reprices bonds at current yields; chains contingencies; rebuts expected objections in advance. Habits: treats non-disclosure as evidence ('the real one sucks'); calls assumptions generous; mixes moral attack with analysis; publishes inline corrections. Sample limits: no discount rate or cost-of-capital framework, and no upside case is modelled. Source: https://www.wheresyoured.at/annualized-enron-rate/
- What Damodaran does with evidence. Sources: his own recurring datasets, FRED, Treasury, S&P Capital IQ, rating agencies, filings, and at times a 'patchwork of public data and leaked information'. Methods: long history for context (1962-2026), decomposition (rate into inflation + real; expected return into riskfree + premium), sector and regional cross-sections with medians, simple day-count tests, toy examples, and story-to-input valuation with a published spreadsheet and a shared sheet for readers' numbers. Habits: states he expects to be wrong, gives bull and bear cases, applies a possible/plausible/probable test, discloses holdings, acknowledges corrections. Limits: key inputs such as 10% failure risk and $3 of revenue per $1 invested are judgments rather than derived values. He calls cost of capital among the least critical inputs, warns against 'conspiracy rabbit holes', and does not trace individual contracts as Zitron does. Source: https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2026/10/the-anthropic-prospectus-arrives-ipo.html

## Still unknown

- Sampling skew: 9 of the 14 pieces date from September 2026 onward and most concern AI. Damodaran's data-update series and his other 2025-26 posts went unread, so these counts cannot represent his full range. Zitron's free output in this period is almost entirely about AI.
- Paywall: Zitron's Premium pieces, including the AI debt guides and 'The Hater's Guide To Junk', were not read. He says his fuller credit analysis is there, so the credit counts may understate what he uses, and his methods there are unknown.
- Count method: frequencies are keyword matches on full text, some of them noisy. For example, 'yield' can mean earnings yield, and 'S&P 500' counts as index exposure. They show appearance, not importance. The dependency arrows and proposed order are my analysis, and neither writer states them.
- Reading depth: Z7 was read in part. Several others had short final passages sampled rather than read line by line.
- The KBRA BB+ rating is unconfirmed in a primary source. An attempt to read the FT article was denied by the session's permission system and I did not retry it. A search limited to kbra.com and sec.gov returned nothing. Its exact date, the instrument rated and when Zitron could have known of it remain unknown.
- Damodaran's Anthropic and country-risk spreadsheets were not opened, so 'close to half a billion' is only presumed to be a slip for trillion, and his $114bn cash figure is unreconciled with the S-1's $112.4bn of cash and cash equivalents; the S-1 also reports short-term investments, and I did not read that amount.
- Near-term compute payments cannot be tested because the S-1 obligations table and run-rate line are blank in this draft. The commitment totals cited by the two writers ($430bn and over $600bn) are not fully reconciled.
- I do not know whether either writer later corrected the slips listed. Pages were read once, on 1 Nov 2026. I did not check Damodaran's non-Treasury data series or the sell-side notes Zitron cites.
- The investment-grade boundary was checked only against Fidelity's table, because the Moody's PDF excerpt did not state the notch boundary. Damodaran's underlying Moody's default table was not seen.
- The six skill definitions are my synthesis. I found no evidence on which definition produces better learning or how reliably a tutor could grade each one.
- Possible bias: this analysis was produced by an Anthropic model, and many checks concern Anthropic's S-1 and criticism of it. The citations and arithmetic can be rechecked, but the framing of the Z5 and D4 comparisons should get an independent read.
