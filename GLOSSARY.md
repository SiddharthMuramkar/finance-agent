# Value It

Value It teaches finance and valuation to beginners, until they can value a company on their own. A Learner studies with the Tutor, one Topic at a time, on real Cases and live Widgets.

## Language

### People and access

**Learner**:
A person who studies in Value It.
_Avoid_: user, student

**Owner**:
The one person who runs Value It and decides who may come in.
_Avoid_: admin

**Visitor**:
Someone on the public site who has not asked to come in.

**Applicant**:
Someone who joined the Waitlist on `/join` and waits for the Owner; Clerk lists them as pending.

**Member**:
An approved person who can study.

**Invite**:
The Owner's advance yes for one email address, so that person skips the wait.

**Approve / Deny**:
The Owner's decision, in Clerk's Waitlist, to let an Applicant in (invite) or keep them out (reject).

**Waitlist**:
The list on `/join` where a Visitor leaves their email to ask in; Clerk keeps it. On screen it has this one name: "Join the waitlist".
_Avoid_: early access list, sign-up list, your place

**Early access**:
The free, invite-only access the site offers: the Owner lets each person in by hand.
_Avoid_: beta

### Tutor and conversation

**Tutor**:
The chatbot that teaches a Learner: it chooses what to show and words each reply.

**Turn**:
One Learner action and the Tutor's whole answer to it.

**Session**:
The whole conversation between one Learner and the Tutor about one Topic.

**Study Workspace**:
Where a signed-in Learner studies: the conversation beside the Widget panel.

**Reply kind**:
What a Tutor reply is for: feedback, rule, worked example, Quick try, re-teach, drill, hint, an answer to the Learner's own question, an offer to park a Skill, Go deeper (the Learner asked for more), or simpler (the Learner asked for it plainer).

**Step-led reply**:
A reply that moves the teaching on at the current Teaching Step.

**Own question**:
A message the Learner typed themselves while no Check waits for an answer.

**Choice**:
A tap-to-answer button under a reply. Pressing it sends its words as the Learner's message.

**Blocked number**:
A number that would give away an open question's answer, so nothing on screen may show it.

**Daily cap**:
The most Tutor Turns and Spend one Learner may use in a day, and the most Spend all Learners together may use. The Owner sets them on `/admin`.

**Spend**:
The Owner's estimate of what one person's Tutor costs.

### Course

**Course Map**:
Every Topic in order, grouped into Subjects.

**Subject**:
A group of related Topics on the Course Map.

**Topic**:
One lesson, built around one idea, its Running example and its Cases.

**Spine**:
The valuation Topics, in a fixed order, that are every new Learner's default path.

**Elective**:
A Topic off the Spine that a Learner opens by choice.

**Primer**:
Short teaching inside a Spine Topic that covers one idea an Elective teaches in full.

**Converted Topic**:
A Topic whose teaching is written ahead, not invented by the Tutor in each Turn.

**Teaching Step**:
One stage of a Topic: moment, concept, explore, your turn, check, reveal, principle.

**Running example**:
The one real event that a Topic's teaching is built around.

**Insight**:
The one idea of a Topic that the Learner finds through play at the explore step, with hints if needed.

**Reveal**:
The moment a Case's outcome is shown. After it, Hindsight is allowed.

**Principle**:
The rule a Topic leaves the Learner with, at its last step.

**Go deeper**:
Optional material beyond a Topic's Checks. It is never quizzed.

### Checks and practice

**Check**:
A test of one idea that the Learner must pass to finish a Topic. A quiz Check is answered by tap; a conversation Check is answered in the Learner's own words.

**Due Check**:
A Check added to a Topic the Learner has already passed, which now waits for them.

**Skill**:
One rule that a Check tests. It is fluent after two right answers in a row.

**Parked Skill**:
A Skill set aside after repeated misses. It holds Your turn until its drill is done.

**Concept card**:
The short written teaching for one Check: a few plain lines, one worked example and one line to write down.

**Quick try**:
A short tap question that practises one Skill and is marked at once.

**Known mistake**:
A common wrong answer, with a one-line fix shown the moment the Learner gives it.

**Skip ahead**:
Passing a whole Topic at once with one mixed quiz.

**Review**:
A question from a passed Topic that is shown again later and answered by tap.

**Question Bank**:
Human-written questions, kept private, that quizzes and Reviews draw from.

**Assignment**:
A task after the Spine: the Learner picks a listed company, finds its numbers and values it as an Own Case.

### Cases and hindsight

**Case**:
A real company or event, studied as it stood at its As-of date.
_Avoid_: example

**As-of date**:
The date a Case stands at, before its outcome was known.

**Hindsight**:
Anything known only after a Case's As-of date.

**As-of rule**:
Until the Reveal, nothing from after a Case's As-of date reaches the Learner.

**Case Data**:
A Case's checked figures as they stood at its As-of date.

**Moment**:
The event a Case or Example is about, told as people knew it on its As-of date.
_Avoid_: story

**Own Case**:
A Case the Learner picks and fills: a listed company, its As-of date (the date of its annual report) and the numbers they found in that report.

**Market reading**:
A market-wide number, such as the premium investors ask over safe bonds, shown with the date it was measured.

**Example**:
A public page where a Visitor plays one Signal widget on a real Moment.
_Avoid_: case, case page

### Library

**Library**:
The store of credited Passages from real Sources that the Tutor searches and cites.

**Source**:
One published work in the Library, with its credit and licence.

**Passage**:
One short cut from a Source, quoted with its credit.

**Citation**:
A link from a claim in a reply to the Passage that supports it.

### Widgets

**Widget**:
A live picture the Learner moves to see an idea. The Tutor chooses it and tested code draws it.

**Widget recipe**:
A tested Widget template that the Tutor fills by choosing its numbers.

**Workbench Widget**:
A Widget in which several controls feed one headline number.

**Lingo card**:
A Widget that explains one Topic term in plain words.

**Companion Widget**:
A Widget added beside a reply that opened none. Today it can only be a Lingo card.

**Widget panel**:
The area beside the conversation that holds the Learner's open Widgets.

**Guided Task**:
An optional small goal inside a Widget, such as finding the break-even.

**Formula Card**:
The only form a named formula takes: each symbol is named in words, and every result comes from tested maths.

**Illustration**:
A Widget or drawing whose numbers are made up, and which is labelled "Illustration".

### Signal

**Signal widget**:
One of the five live Widgets the site shows: Jackpot, Four levers, Compounding, Rates, WeWork.

**Call**:
The answer the Learner commits to in a Widget before the Widget shows its own. The controls unlock after it.

**Verdict**:
Whether a Call is right. It comes from checked data, never from the Tutor.

**Milestone**:
A moment in a Widget worth naming, such as finding the break-even or reaching the Price.

**Lever**:
One of the four drivers of a valuation: growth, margin, reinvestment and risk.

**Story**:
What someone claims about a company's future: a founder, an analyst, a headline or the Price itself. A Learner judges it against what happened.
_Avoid_: your story

**Valuation story**:
A Story told as settings of the four Levers, which gives a value per share.

**Reference story**:
The published Valuation story a levers Widget starts from, such as Damodaran's Amazon of February 2000.

**Estimate**:
A Learner's own Lever settings for an Own Case, made in an Assignment.
_Avoid_: your story

**Full sheet**:
Every input of a valuation in plain words, behind Go deeper. The four Levers are its front.

**Price**:
What the market paid on the As-of date. It is shown against value, never as right or wrong.

**Colour language**:
Five colours and one pattern, each with one meaning: You, Gain, Loss, Careful, The record, Not real yet.

### Notebook and memory

**Notebook**:
The Learner's own saved lines, replies and Widgets.

**Topic Note**:
The Notebook page for one Topic: what was found, the slips and their fixes, and the Learner's own lines.

**Learner summary**:
A short note of what the Tutor knows about a Learner across Topics.

## Relationships

- A **Visitor** who asks to come in becomes an **Applicant**; **Approve** makes them a **Member**, **Deny** keeps them out. An **Invite** makes a person a **Member** with no wait.
- A **Session** belongs to one **Learner** and one **Topic**, and holds many **Turns**.
- The **Course Map** groups every **Topic** into **Subjects**. Every **Topic** is either on the **Spine** or an **Elective**.
- A **Topic** has one **Running example**, its **Teaching Steps**, one or more **Checks** and its **Cases**.
- Each **Check** has one **Concept card** and tests its **Skills**; **Quick tries** practise one **Skill** each.
- A **Case** has one **As-of date**; its **Reveal** ends the **As-of rule** for it.
- The **Library** holds **Sources**; a **Source** holds **Passages**; a **Citation** points at one **Passage**.
- A **Valuation story** is a **Story** told as all four **Levers**; a **Reference story** is a published **Valuation story**.
- On the **Spine** the **Learner** judges other people's **Stories**. Their own **Estimate** comes only in an **Assignment**, which asks for one **Own Case**.
- A **Case** and an **Example** each tell one **Moment**.
- The **Notebook** holds one **Topic Note** per **Topic** the **Learner** studied.

## Flagged ambiguities

- **Case** and **Example** are different things: a **Case** is studied in a **Topic**; an **Example** is a public page with one **Signal widget**. A **Running example** is the event inside one **Topic**, not an **Example**.
- **Story** and **Moment** are different things: a **Story** is a claim about the future that the **Learner** judges; a **Moment** is the event itself. "The story" never labels the event.
- **Estimate** and **Valuation story** both set the **Levers**: a **Valuation story** is someone else's, judged on the **Spine**; an **Estimate** is the **Learner**'s own, made only in an **Assignment**.
- **Check**, **Quick try** and **Review** are all questions: only a **Check** must be passed to finish a **Topic**; a **Quick try** practises; a **Review** comes back after the **Topic** is passed.
- **Learner**, **Applicant** and **Member** name one person at different times: an **Applicant** waits, a **Member** may study, and a **Learner** is someone who studies.
